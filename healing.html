Toolkit 解压方法

Eight that work without an app, a subscription or a good mood.

  • Physiological sigh — two short inhales through the nose, then one long exhale. Three rounds. The fastest way down that needs nothing but air.
  • Cold on the wrists — thirty seconds of cold water on the inside of the wrists or the face. The dive reflex slows the heart before the mind can argue.
  • Ten minutes outside, no headphones — walking without input is what lets a loop finish itself. Leave the phone in the pocket.
  • Name it in one sentence — “I am angry that the deadline moved.” Labelling an emotion measurably lowers its grip; vague dread does not.
  • One tab — close everything except the thing you are actually doing. The mess on screen is a tax you pay every glance.
  • Shoulders, jaw, hands — drop the shoulders, unclench the jaw, open the fingers. Twenty seconds; most tension lives in those three places.
  • Write it out for five minutes — paper or the box below. Not a plan, not a message: just the noise, until the page stops being interesting.
  • Sunlight in the first hour — ten minutes outdoors early sets the day's rhythm, and a settled rhythm does more for mood than most apps.
