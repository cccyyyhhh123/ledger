What is honest here

  • Video wallpapers — a plain .mp4/.webm in the project folder. Capped with ffmpeg to 1280 wide and 30 fps, no audio, so a 593 MB source becomes about 4 MB and the page stays cheap.
  • Scene wallpapers — Wallpaper Engine keeps the geometry, puppet meshes, particles and SceneScript inside a closed .pkg rendered by its own engine. There is no MIT engine called WebWallGL; the closest open reimplementation is a native Linux renderer, and nothing of that runs in WebGL without a rewrite of the whole scene format. So these fall back to their preview frame, which is what the library shows.
  • Sound — a browser cannot read the system output. The bars are drawn from the page's own audio graph, or from a tab you explicitly share with the audio checkbox. Now Playing on the desk comes from the music bridge, not from here.
  • Power — the video pauses when the tab is hidden, when the window is not focused, and when the stage is scrolled out of view.
